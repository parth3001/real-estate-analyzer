## What's Included in Phase 1

### Supported Property Types

**Phase 1 focuses on commercial multi-family properties with 5+ units**:

- ✅ **5-32 Units**: Recommended range with highest data accuracy
- ✅ **Garden-Style Apartments**: Most common building type (60% of market)
- ✅ **Mid-Rise with Elevators**: Institutional-grade buildings (4-9 stories)
- ✅ **Multi-Building Complexes**: Campus-style developments with shared amenities

### Why 5+ Units?

**Commercial multi-family properties (5+ units) are fundamentally different from small multi-family (2-4 units)**:

1. **Financing**: Commercial loans vs residential mortgages
2. **Metrics**: DSCR and Cap Rate replace traditional mortgage metrics
3. **Operating Expenses**: Professional property management required
4. **Valuation**: Income approach vs comparable sales approach
5. **Tax Treatment**: Different depreciation schedules and deductions

### Not Included in Phase 1

❌ **2-4 Unit Properties**: Use SFR Analyzer instead (see below)
❌ **High-Rise (10+ stories)**: Future phase for institutional assets
❌ **Student Housing**: Specialized analysis coming in future phase
❌ **Senior Living**: Specialized analysis coming in future phase
❌ **Mixed-Use**: Commercial + residential requires different approach

## Why 2-4 Units Should Use SFR Analyzer

### Critical Difference: Residential vs Commercial Financing

**2-4 unit properties qualify for RESIDENTIAL financing**:

- ✅ FHA loans available (3.5% down payment)
- ✅ Conventional loans with 15-25% down payment
- ✅ Owner-occupancy programs (house hacking)
- ✅ Lower interest rates (residential mortgage rates)
- ✅ Easier qualification criteria (debt-to-income ratios)

**5+ unit properties require COMMERCIAL financing**:

- 🏢 Commercial loans only (25-35% down payment)
- 🏢 Higher interest rates (0.5-1.5% above residential)
- 🏢 Balloon payments and shorter terms (5-10 years)
- 🏢 Stricter qualification (DSCR requirements)
- 🏢 More complex underwriting process

### How to Analyze 2-4 Unit Properties

**Use the SFR (Single-Family Rental) Analyzer** for 2-4 unit properties:

1. Navigate to **SFR Property Analysis** in the main menu
2. Enter property details as you would for a single-family home
3. Enter rental income for ALL units combined (e.g., 3-unit = $3,000/month total)
4. The analyzer will use residential financing assumptions
5. Results will show traditional mortgage metrics (loan-to-value, monthly payment)

**Example**:
- **3-Unit Property**: Purchase $350K, Total Rent $3,000/month
- **Use SFR Analyzer**: Enter as single property with $3,000 monthly rent
- **Financing**: 20% down, 7% interest rate, 30-year fixed mortgage

## Building Type Selection Guide

### Why Building Type Matters

**Building type affects THREE critical aspects of your analysis**:

1. **Operating Expenses**: Different maintenance, insurance, and utility costs
2. **Cap Rate Targets**: Institutional investors pay premiums for certain building types
3. **Data Validation**: System alerts you if expenses seem unusual for your building type

### The Three Building Types

---

#### 🏘️ GARDEN (Most Common - 60% of Market)

**Visual Description**:
- 2-3 story buildings with outdoor corridors
- Surface parking (no structured parking)
- Walk-up access (no elevators required)
- Wood-frame or brick construction
- Individual HVAC units per apartment

**Typical Property Profile**:
- 5-50 units per building
- Built 1970-present
- Suburban or urban infill locations
- Class B or C properties

**Operating Expenses**: **$250-400/unit/month**

**Key Expense Drivers**:
- Lower insurance (no elevator liability)
- Simple maintenance (no elevator, no parking structure)
- Individual utilities (tenants pay most)
- Moderate landscaping costs

**Cap Rate Target**: **Market Baseline** (no adjustment)

**Best For**:
- First-time multi-family investors
- Value-add opportunities
- Cash flow focused investors
- Markets with strong rental demand

---

#### 🏢 MID_RISE (Institutional Grade - 25% of Market)

**Visual Description**:
- 4-9 story buildings with interior corridors
- Elevator required (ADA compliance)
- Structured parking (garage or covered)
- Concrete or steel-frame construction
- Central HVAC systems

**Typical Property Profile**:
- 30-150 units per building
- Built 1990-present
- Urban core or high-density suburbs
- Class A or B properties

**Operating Expenses**: **$450-700/unit/month**

**Key Expense Drivers**:
- **Elevator maintenance**: $1,200-2,000/month per elevator
- Higher insurance (elevator, parking structure)
- Central HVAC maintenance
- Professional management required
- Structured parking maintenance

**Cap Rate Target**: **-150 basis points** (1.5% lower than GARDEN)

**Why Lower Cap Rate?**:
- Institutional investors (REITs, pension funds) prefer mid-rise
- Better rent premiums ($1.50-2.50/sqft vs $1.00-1.50/sqft for garden)
- Longer lifespan (60+ years vs 40 years for garden)
- Lower turnover (higher-end tenants)

**Best For**:
- Experienced investors
- Urban markets with strong job growth
- Long-term hold strategies
- Institutional exit opportunities

---

#### 🏘️ COMPLEX (Multi-Building Campus - 15% of Market)

**Visual Description**:
- Multiple garden-style buildings on shared campus
- Centralized amenities (pool, clubhouse, gym)
- Large parking areas (200+ spaces)
- Professional landscaping and maintenance
- May include garages or carports

**Typical Property Profile**:
- 25-200+ units across 3-10 buildings
- Built 1980-present
- Suburban locations with land availability
- Class B or C properties

**Operating Expenses**: **$300-500/unit/month**

**Key Expense Drivers**:
- Larger landscaping and grounds maintenance
- Shared amenity costs (pool, clubhouse)
- Parking lot maintenance (larger surface areas)
- Higher utility costs (common area lighting)
- Potential on-site management office

**Cap Rate Target**: **Market Baseline** (no adjustment)

**Best For**:
- Investors seeking scale
- Suburban markets
- Value-add repositioning opportunities
- Markets with land availability

---

### How to Choose Your Building Type

**Ask Yourself These Questions**:

1. **How many stories is the building?**
   - 2-3 stories with outdoor corridors → **GARDEN**
   - 4-9 stories with elevators → **MID_RISE**
   - Multiple 2-3 story buildings → **COMPLEX**

2. **Is there an elevator?**
   - No elevator → **GARDEN** or **COMPLEX**
   - Elevator required → **MID_RISE**

3. **How many buildings on the property?**
   - Single building → **GARDEN** or **MID_RISE**
   - Multiple buildings with shared amenities → **COMPLEX**

4. **What type of parking?**
   - Surface parking → **GARDEN** or **COMPLEX**
   - Structured parking (garage) → **MID_RISE**

**Still Not Sure?** Select **GARDEN** as the default. The system will validate your operating expenses and alert you if they seem unusual for a garden-style building.

## Operating Expense Expectations

### What Are Operating Expenses?

**Operating expenses include ALL costs to maintain and operate the property** (excluding mortgage payment):

✅ Property taxes
✅ Insurance (building and liability)
✅ Utilities (water, sewer, trash, common area electric)
✅ Maintenance and repairs
✅ Property management fees
✅ Landscaping and grounds
✅ Pest control
✅ Snow removal (if applicable)
✅ Marketing and leasing costs

❌ Mortgage payment (NOT an operating expense)
❌ Capital improvements (e.g., new roof, HVAC replacement)

### Operating Expense Ranges by Building Type

| Building Type | Per Unit/Month | Annual (12-unit example) | Key Drivers |
|---------------|----------------|---------------------------|-------------|
| **GARDEN** | $250-400 | $36,000-57,600 | Lower insurance, simple maintenance |
| **MID_RISE** | $450-700 | $64,800-100,800 | Elevator, structured parking, higher insurance |
| **COMPLEX** | $300-500 | $43,200-72,000 | Large grounds, shared amenities |

### Why These Ranges?

**GARDEN ($250-400/unit/month)**:
- Property taxes: $100-150/unit/month
- Insurance: $30-50/unit/month (no elevator reduces liability)
- Utilities: $40-60/unit/month (common area only)
- Maintenance: $40-80/unit/month (simple systems)
- Management: $40-60/unit/month (8-10% of rent)

**MID_RISE ($450-700/unit/month)**:
- Property taxes: $150-200/unit/month (higher property values)
- Insurance: $60-100/unit/month (elevator, parking structure)
- Utilities: $60-100/unit/month (central HVAC, elevators)
- Maintenance: $100-150/unit/month (elevator $40-65/unit, HVAC, parking)
- Management: $80-150/unit/month (8-10% of higher rents)

**COMPLEX ($300-500/unit/month)**:
- Property taxes: $100-150/unit/month
- Insurance: $40-70/unit/month
- Utilities: $50-80/unit/month (larger common areas)
- Maintenance: $60-100/unit/month (grounds, amenities)
- Management: $50-100/unit/month

### Validation Warnings

**The system will alert you if your operating expenses are outside these ranges**:

⚠️ **LOW Severity**: 10-20% below typical range
⚠️ **MEDIUM Severity**: 20-40% below typical range
⚠️ **HIGH Severity**: 40%+ below typical range

**Example Warning**:
```
Severity: MEDIUM
Category: Operating Expenses
Message: Operating expenses ($200/unit/month) appear low for GARDEN building
Impact: Actual expenses may be $4,800 higher annually
Recommendation: Typical range for GARDEN: $250-400/unit/month. Verify all expense categories are included.
```

**What to Do**:
1. Double-check you've included ALL expense categories
2. Compare to actual property financials (T-12 statement)
3. Factor in deferred maintenance (catch-up repairs)
4. Consider market-specific factors (e.g., high property taxes in TX)

## Cap Rate Benchmarks by Building Type

### What is a Cap Rate?

**Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate)** = Annual Net Operating Income ÷ Purchase Price

- **Higher Cap Rate**: Higher cash flow return, but often higher risk
- **Lower Cap Rate**: Lower cash flow return, but often lower risk and more stability

### Cap Rate Targets by Market Tier

| Market Tier | Example Cities | GARDEN | MID_RISE | COMPLEX |
|-------------|----------------|---------|----------|---------|
| **A-Class** (Premium) | Dallas, Austin, Seattle | 5.0% | 3.5% | 5.0% |
| **B-Class** (Balanced) | Phoenix, Tampa, Nashville | 7.5% | 6.0% | 7.5% |
| **C-Class** (Cash Flow) | Memphis, Birmingham, Detroit | 10.0% | 8.5% | 10.0% |

### Why Mid-Rise Has Lower Cap Rates

**Mid-rise buildings command a -150 basis point (1.5%) premium** because:

1. **Institutional Appeal**: REITs and pension funds prefer mid-rise for scale
2. **Rent Premiums**: Mid-rise tenants pay $1.50-2.50/sqft vs $1.00-1.50 for garden
3. **Longer Lifespan**: 60+ years for concrete/steel vs 40 years for wood-frame
4. **Lower Turnover**: Higher-end tenants stay longer (lower vacancy costs)
5. **Urban Core Locations**: Better job access and walkability

### Walk-Away Price Example

**Property**: 20-unit building, $100,000 annual NOI, Phoenix market (B-Class)

| Building Type | Cap Rate Target | Walk-Away Price | Price Difference |
|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|------------------|
| **GARDEN** | 7.5% | $1,333,333 | Baseline |
| **MID_RISE** | 6.0% | $1,666,667 | **+$333,334** |
| **COMPLEX** | 7.5% | $1,333,333 | Same as GARDEN |

**Key Insight**: You can pay $333K MORE for a mid-rise building with the same NOI because institutional investors will pay that premium when you sell.

## Understanding Validation Warnings

### What Are Validation Warnings?

**Validation warnings are NON-BLOCKING alerts** that help you identify potential data quality issues:

✅ **They do NOT stop your analysis** - you get results immediately
✅ **They highlight potential problems** - unusual expenses, financing risks, data gaps
✅ **They provide recommendations** - actionable steps to improve analysis accuracy

### Four Categories of Warnings

#### 1️⃣ OPERATING_EXPENSES

**What We Check**:
- Are expenses within typical range for your building type?
- Are expenses unusually low (missing categories)?
- Are expenses unusually high (verify actual costs)?

**Example Warning**:
```
Severity: MEDIUM
Message: Operating expenses ($200/unit/month) appear low for GARDEN building
Impact: Actual expenses may be $4,800 higher annually
Recommendation: Typical range: $250-400/unit/month. Verify all categories included.
```

#### 2️⃣ FINANCING

**What We Check**:
- Is DSCR above lender minimum (1.20-1.25)?
- Is down payment within commercial norms (25-35%)?
- Are financing assumptions realistic for property type?

**Example Warning**:
```
Severity: HIGH
Message: DSCR of 1.10 is below most lender requirements (1.20-1.25 minimum)
Impact: Loan may not be approved without larger down payment
Recommendation: Increase down payment to 30% to improve DSCR to 1.25
```

#### 3️⃣ MARKET_DATA

**What We Check**:
- Is market data recent (within 6 months)?
- Are comparable properties similar in size/type?
- Is rent data from reliable sources?

**Example Warning**:
```
Severity: LOW
Message: Market rent data is 4 months old
Impact: Current market rents may be 2-5% higher
Recommendation: Verify current market rents with local property manager
```

#### 4️⃣ INPUT_VALIDATION

**What We Check**:
- Are all required fields completed?
- Are values within reasonable ranges?
- Is data internally consistent?

**Example Warning**:
```
Severity: MEDIUM
Message: Current rent ($800/unit) is 20% below market rent ($1,000/unit)
Impact: Potential $48,000 annual upside if rents brought to market
Recommendation: Consider value-add strategy to increase rents
```

### Severity Levels

**🟢 LOW**: Informational, minor concern
- Review when you have time
- May indicate opportunity (e.g., below-market rents)
- No immediate action required

**🟡 MEDIUM**: Should investigate, may affect results
- Review before making offer
- Could impact cash flow by 10-20%
- Consider getting professional validation

**🔴 HIGH**: Critical issue, strongly affects results
- Review immediately
- Could impact deal viability
- May prevent financing approval

### What to Do with Warnings

1. **Read the Message**: Understand what the system flagged
2. **Review the Impact**: How much could this affect your analysis?
3. **Follow the Recommendation**: Specific action steps provided
4. **Update Your Data**: Re-run analysis with corrected data
5. **Consult Professionals**: Property manager, lender, CPA for complex issues

## Key Metrics for Multi-Family Analysis

### Primary Metrics (Most Important)

#### 1️⃣ Cap Rate (Capitalization Rate)

**Formula**: Annual NOI ÷ Purchase Price

**What It Tells You**: What return you'd earn if you paid all cash

**Target Ranges**:
- A-Class Markets: 4-6%
- B-Class Markets: 6-8%
- C-Class Markets: 8-12%

**Example**: $100K NOI, $1.5M purchase → 6.67% cap rate

---

#### 2️⃣ DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio)

**Formula**: Annual NOI ÷ Annual Debt Service

**What It Tells You**: How easily you can cover the mortgage payment

**Lender Requirements**:
- Fannie Mae: 1.25x minimum
- Freddie Mac: 1.20x minimum
- HUD: 1.18x minimum
- Commercial Banks: 1.25-1.35x

**Example**: $100K NOI, $75K annual mortgage → 1.33 DSCR ✅

---

#### 3️⃣ Cash-on-Cash Return

**Formula**: Annual Cash Flow ÷ Total Cash Invested

**What It Tells You**: What return you earn on your actual cash investment

**Target Ranges**:
- Conservative: 6-8%
- Moderate: 8-12%
- Aggressive: 12%+

**Example**: $25K annual cash flow, $400K down payment → 6.25% CoC return

---

### Secondary Metrics (Supporting Analysis)

#### 4️⃣ Gross Rent Multiplier (GRM)

**Formula**: Purchase Price ÷ Gross Annual Rent

**Target**: 4-7 for residential multi-family

**What It Tells You**: Quick valuation check (similar to price-to-earnings ratio)

---

#### 5️⃣ Debt Yield

**Formula**: Annual NOI ÷ Loan Amount

**Lender Requirement**: 10%+ for approval

**What It Tells You**: Lender's risk if they foreclose (how fast they recover)

---

#### 6️⃣ Break-Even Occupancy (BEO)

**Formula**: (Operating Expenses + Debt Service) ÷ Gross Potential Rent

**Target**: 60-75% for stable properties

**What It Tells You**: What occupancy you need to break even (cover all costs)

---

### Per-Unit Metrics

**Why Per-Unit?**: Allows comparison across different property sizes

- **NOI per Unit**: $3,000-8,000/year typical
- **Cash Flow per Unit**: $1,000-4,000/year typical
- **Operating Expenses per Unit**: $250-700/month depending on building type

## Common Questions

### Q1: Should I enter unit-level data or use unit types?

**Answer**: Use **unit-level data** if you have it (best accuracy):
- Enter each unit's rent, sqft, bedrooms
- System calculates weighted averages
- Best for properties with rent rolls

**Use unit types if you don't have detailed data**:
- Enter unit mix (e.g., 8 x 2BR @ $1,200, 4 x 1BR @ $900)
- System calculates totals
- Good for initial analysis or listings without rent rolls

---

### Q2: What if I don't know the building type?

**Answer**: Select **GARDEN** as the default (60% of market is garden-style). The system will validate your operating expenses and alert you if they seem unusual for a garden building.

---

### Q3: How accurate are the cap rate targets?

**Answer**: Cap rate targets are based on:
- Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac underwriting standards
- CBRE, Marcus & Millichap market reports (2023-2024)
- National Multi Housing Council benchmarks

**Accuracy**: 90-95% for typical markets. Adjust for:
- Micro-markets (specific neighborhoods may vary)
- Property condition (deferred maintenance)
- Local regulations (rent control, zoning changes)

---

### Q4: Can I use this for 2-4 unit properties?

**Answer**: No, use the **SFR Analyzer** for 2-4 unit properties. They qualify for residential financing with different metrics and assumptions.

---

### Q5: What if my operating expenses are higher than the range?

**Answer**: High operating expenses are NOT flagged (conservative is good). Low expenses ARE flagged because they may indicate:
- Missing expense categories
- Deferred maintenance not accounted for
- Seller underreporting expenses

---

### Q6: Do I need to include property management fees?

**Answer**: **YES, always include 8-10% for property management**, even if you plan to self-manage:
- Your time has value
- Future buyer will hire a manager (affects resale value)
- Professional standard for institutional analysis

---

### Q7: How does building type affect my walk-away price?

**Answer**:
- **GARDEN**: Standard cap rate target
- **MID_RISE**: You can pay 1.5% MORE (lower cap rate = higher price for same NOI)
- **COMPLEX**: Standard cap rate target

**Example** (Phoenix B-Class, $100K NOI):
- GARDEN: $1,333K walk-away
- MID_RISE: $1,667K walk-away (+$334K premium)
- COMPLEX: $1,333K walk-away

---

### Q8: What's the difference between NOI and Cash Flow?

**Answer**:
- **NOI (Net Operating Income)**: Income - Operating Expenses (BEFORE mortgage)
- **Cash Flow**: NOI - Mortgage Payment (AFTER mortgage)

**NOI** is used for valuation (cap rate calculation)
**Cash Flow** is what you actually keep each month

---

### Q9: Can I change the building type after I start?

**Answer**: Yes, you can change building type anytime:
1. Update building type in property details
2. Re-run analysis
3. System recalculates cap rate targets and validation ranges

---

### Q10: What if I disagree with a validation warning?

**Answer**: Validation warnings are **educational, not prescriptive**:
- If you have actual property financials (T-12 statement), use those
- If you know market-specific factors (e.g., low property taxes), ignore warning
- Warnings help beginners avoid common mistakes, but you're in control

## Next Steps

### ✅ Ready to Analyze Your First Multi-Family Property?

1. **Gather Your Data**:
   - Property address
   - Purchase price
   - Unit count and rent roll (if available)
   - Operating expenses (or use our benchmarks)
   - Financing terms (down payment, interest rate)

2. **Choose Your Building Type**:
   - Review the guide above
   - Select GARDEN, MID_RISE, or COMPLEX
   - System validates your inputs

3. **Run Your Analysis**:
   - Enter data in Property Wizard (4 steps)
   - Get instant results with investment verdict
   - Review validation warnings (if any)

4. **Make Your Decision**:
   - BUY: Strong deal, meets all criteria
   - NEGOTIATE: Good deal if you can improve price/terms
   - CAUTION: Marginal deal, proceed carefully
   - PASS: Does not meet investment criteria
