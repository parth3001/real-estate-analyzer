## What Is the BRRRR Strategy?

BRRRR is a real estate investment strategy where you buy a distressed property below market value, renovate it to increase its worth, rent it out to a tenant, refinance based on the new higher value to pull your original investment back out, and then repeat the entire process with that same capital.

The key difference between BRRRR and a standard rental purchase is capital recycling. With a traditional buy-and-hold, your down payment sits locked in one property forever. With BRRRR, you recover that capital through the refinance and redeploy it into the next deal — theoretically building a portfolio without needing fresh cash for every acquisition.

BRRRR works best for investors who want to scale a rental portfolio without raising new capital for each property. It rewards people who can accurately estimate rehab costs and after-repair values, and who have the patience to execute a 12-16 month cycle.

Here's the thing most BRRRR content won't tell you: the concept is simple, but the math is where deals live or die.

## The 6 BRRRR Numbers That Determine If a Deal Works

Every BRRRR deal comes down to six metrics. Miss one and you could end up with a property that looked great on a napkin but bleeds cash in reality. Let's walk through each one using a real example: a $150K purchase, $30K rehab, $220K ARV, renting at $1,400/month, with a 75% LTV refinance at 7% interest.

### 1. Capital Recovery Percentage

**What it is:** The percentage of your total cash investment you get back when you refinance.

**Formula:** Cash recovered from refinance ÷ Total cash invested × 100

This is the single most important BRRRR metric because it determines whether the strategy actually works — whether you get your capital back to do it again. A deal with great cash flow but 50% capital recovery defeats the entire purpose of BRRRR.

**Example:** Your total investment is $150K (purchase) + $30K (rehab) + $3K (closing costs) = $183K. Refinance at 75% LTV on a $220K ARV gives you a $165K loan. That means you recover $165K of your cash invested, leaving $18K still in the deal. That's 81% capital recovery — solid, but not infinite return territory.

Now consider: if the ARV were $260K instead, 75% LTV = $195K loan. You'd recover more than you invested. That's infinite return territory.

**Benchmark:** 80% or higher is strong. 100% or above means you recovered all your capital and the deal funds itself.

### 2. Cash-on-Cash Return

**What it is:** Your annual cash flow as a percentage of the cash you have left in the deal after refinance.

**Formula:** Annual cash flow ÷ Cash remaining in deal × 100

Cash-on-cash return tells you how hard your trapped capital is working. The less cash left in the deal and the higher the cash flow, the better this number looks. In infinite return scenarios (where you've pulled all cash out), this metric becomes infinite — which is the whole point.

**Benchmark:** 8–12% is the institutional standard. Below 8% and you should ask whether a simpler buy-and-hold would give you better risk-adjusted returns.

### 3. DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio)

**What it is:** How much income the property generates relative to its debt payments.

**Formula:** Net Operating Income ÷ Annual debt payments

Lenders use DSCR to determine whether a property can pay its own mortgage. Below 1.0 means the property doesn't generate enough income to cover the debt — you're subsidizing it out of pocket every month. Most DSCR lenders won't approve a loan below 1.0, and many require 1.25 or higher.

**Benchmark:** 1.25 or above for most lenders. Below 1.0 is a hard stop.

### 4. The 70% Rule

**What it is:** A quick-filter formula to determine the maximum you should pay for a BRRRR property.

**Formula:** ARV × 0.70 − Rehab costs = Maximum purchase price

**Example:** $220K ARV × 0.70 = $154K − $30K rehab = $124K maximum purchase price. A property purchased at $150K fails this test by $26K — a signal that this deal may not leave enough margin for a successful BRRRR.

The 70% rule is a screening tool, not a hard rule. In hot markets where distressed inventory is scarce, investors sometimes stretch to 75%. But loosening this rule is how marginal deals sneak through your filters.

### 5. Monthly Cash Flow

**What it is:** The money left in your pocket each month after every expense is paid.

**Formula:** Rent − Mortgage − Taxes − Insurance − Maintenance − Vacancy reserve

**Benchmark:** $200/door minimum is the standard floor. Many experienced investors won't touch anything under $300/door. At current 7-8% interest rates, achieving positive post-refi cash flow on many deals requires either a lower purchase price or higher rents.

### 6. Infinite Return

**What it is:** The scenario where your capital recovery exceeds 100% — you pull out more cash than you put in.

This is the holy grail of the BRRRR strategy. When you achieve infinite return, you own a cash-flowing rental property with zero of your own capital remaining in the deal.

**Why it matters:** Infinite return means the deal funds the next one. This is how investors scale from 1 property to 10 without needing 10x the capital.

## The 4 Most Common BRRRR Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)

### Overestimating ARV

This is the mistake that kills more BRRRR deals than anything else. Investors use Zillow Zestimates, cherry-pick the highest comp, or use sales from 6+ months ago in a shifting market. The fix: pull 3 comparable sales within 0.5 miles, same bedroom and bathroom count, sold within the last 90 days. Average the price per square foot across all three and multiply by your property's square footage. If you can't find 3 tight comps, your ARV estimate isn't reliable enough to underwrite a BRRRR.

### Underestimating Rehab Costs

First-time rehabbers consistently underestimate costs by 25–30%. Plumbing surprises, electrical upgrades, permit delays, and contractor change orders add up fast. The rule: add 15–20% contingency to every rehab estimate, no exceptions. If your contractor quotes $30K, underwrite the deal at $36K. If the deal only works at $30K, it doesn't work.

### Ignoring Seasoning Requirements

Most lenders require 6–12 months of ownership before they'll approve a cash-out refinance. That means you're carrying the property for months before you can execute the refinance. Factor these holding costs into your total investment calculation. A deal that looks profitable based on purchase + rehab alone might lose its margin once you add 8 months of carrying costs.

### Forgetting Vacancy in Cash Flow

An 8–10% vacancy rate is standard for most rental markets. On a $1,400/month property, 8% vacancy means $112/month in lost income — money that never hits your bank account. This single adjustment can flip a deal from cash flow positive to negative. Always underwrite with vacancy. If the deal doesn't work at 8% vacancy, it doesn't work.

## BRRRR vs. Buy and Hold: Which Strategy Is Right for You?

BRRRR is the better strategy for scaling quickly. You recycle capital, build a portfolio faster, and can achieve infinite returns on individual deals. But it requires rehab expertise (or a reliable contractor), accurate ARV estimation, and comfort with a 12–16 month execution timeline with multiple moving parts.

Buy and hold is simpler. You buy a rent-ready property, place a tenant, and collect cash flow. Less risk, fewer variables, and no rehab headaches. The tradeoff: your capital stays locked in each property, so scaling requires fresh cash for every acquisition.

**When BRRRR makes sense:** You have rehab experience or a trusted contractor, your market has undervalued distressed properties available, and you want to scale beyond 2–3 properties without raising new capital each time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a good BRRRR capital recovery percentage?**
80% or higher is strong. 100% or above (infinite return) is the goal of the strategy. Below 70% means too much capital is trapped for BRRRR to work as a scaling strategy — at that point, you're better off treating it as a standard buy-and-hold.

**How is BRRRR different from house flipping?**
Flipping sells the property after rehab for a one-time profit. BRRRR refinances and holds it as a rental — you keep the appreciating asset, collect monthly cash flow, and recycle the capital. Flipping creates income; BRRRR creates wealth.

**What LTV should I target for the BRRRR refinance?**
Most conventional lenders offer 75% LTV on investment properties. Some DSCR lenders go to 80%. Use 75% for conservative projections — if the deal works at 75%, it works.

**How do I estimate ARV accurately?**
Pull 3 sold comparable properties within 0.5 miles, matching bedroom and bathroom count, sold within the last 90 days. Average the price per square foot across all three comps and multiply by your property's square footage. If tight comps don't exist, your ARV estimate isn't reliable.

**What does infinite return mean in BRRRR?**
When your refinance returns more cash than you originally invested, your remaining capital in the deal is zero or negative. Any return divided by $0 invested equals infinity. This is the BRRRR holy grail — you own a cash-flowing asset with none of your own money tied up.

**Is the BRRRR strategy risky?**
More than a standard rental purchase, yes. The main risks are ARV overestimation, rehab cost blowouts, interest rates rising before your refinance, and lender seasoning requirements delaying your exit. Conservative underwriting — using real comps, adding rehab contingency, and stress-testing at higher rates — mitigates most of these risks.

**What credit score do I need for a BRRRR refinance?**
Conventional lenders typically require 680+ minimum and offer the best rates at 720+. DSCR loan programs may approve at 660+, though 700+ gets better terms and lower origination fees.

**How long does a full BRRRR cycle take?**
Expect 2–4 months for rehab, then 6–12 months of seasoning before the refinance. Total cycle: 8–16 months from purchase to refinance completion. Plan your capital allocation around this timeline — your cash is locked up until the refinance closes.
